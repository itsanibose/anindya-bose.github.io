About

The engineer who stayed close to the hard problems.

I started my career in enterprise data long before "data platform" was a common phrase — when the hard work was making SAP ecosystems talk to anything, and making sense of the data that came out. Over eighteen years, the tools changed dramatically. The core challenge did not: how do you build something that is both technically sound and organizationally trusted?

Today, I lead Data and AI Engineering initiatives where that question is more pressing than ever. My work sits at the intersection of modern cloud infrastructure and mission-critical enterprise systems — Azure Databricks lakehouses built on top of SAP S/4HANA and BW/4HANA sources, governed through Unity Catalog, and increasingly extended with applied AI: RAG pipelines, LLM-powered data products, and agentic workflows that have to perform reliably in regulated, production-grade environments.

I have led cross-functional teams of twenty to over a hundred engineers across geographies, at organizations like Capgemini and Becton Dickinson. The scale taught me that the most consequential skill in this field is not technical — it is the ability to hold technical rigor and human complexity in the same hand, at the same time.
